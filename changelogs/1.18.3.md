## [1.18.3] - 2026-10-29
### Added
Two quotes added to Great Speeches:
- **speech-038**: Lincoln's "A house divided against itself cannot stand..."
  from the "House Divided" speech (Illinois Republican State Convention,
  Springfield, June 16, 1858), confirmed against the National Park Service's
  text of *The Collected Works of Abraham Lincoln*.
- **speech-039**: Reagan's closing lines on the Challenger disaster ("...slipped
  the surly bonds of earth..."), confirmed against the Reagan Presidential
  Library transcript of the Oval Office address (January 28, 1986).

Six more quotes added to existing collections:
- **Edgar Allan Poe**: poe-032, "Deep into that darkness peering, long I stood
  there wondering, fearing..." from *The Raven* (1845).
- **Jane Austen**: austen-038, "Seldom, very seldom does complete truth belong
  to any human disclosure..." from *Emma*, Vol. III, Ch. XIII (1815).
- **Thoreau & Walden**: thoreau-031, "But lo! men have become the tools of their
  tools," from "Economy", and thoreau-032, "The universe is wider than our views
  of it," from "Conclusion", both from *Walden* (1854).
- **Entrepreneurs**: entre-031, the "faster horse" line usually credited to
  Henry Ford, as `unverified`: no Ford source has been found, and its first
  known appearance is a 2001 letter to *Marketing Week*, long after Ford's death.
- **One-Liners**: oneliner-030, "A lie can travel halfway around the world
  while the truth is putting on its shoes," as a proverb rather than Mark
  Twain's: the earliest known form is Jonathan Swift's "Falsehood flies, and the
  Truth comes limping after it" in *The Examiner* (1710).

### Fixed
- **Great Scientists**: sci-027 (Heisenberg) now reads "nature itself" rather
  than "nature in itself", per *Physics and Philosophy* (1958). sci-028
  (McClintock) now reads "I know them intimately..." rather than "my corn
  plants", per Evelyn Fox Keller's *A Feeling for the Organism* (1983, p. 198).
- **Great Speeches**: speech-023 (Susan B. Anthony) now reads "...who formed
  this Union" rather than "the Union", per the text of her April 3, 1873
  address on Voices of Democracy.

- **Great Speeches**: speech-031 (Elie Wiesel) now reads "Wherever men and
  women are persecuted" rather than "men or women", per his 1986 Nobel Peace
  Prize acceptance speech.
- **Grit & Perseverance**: grit-031, Theodore Roosevelt's "It is not the critic
  who counts", now carries the 1910 printed text of "Citizenship in a Republic"
  (*African and European Addresses*), as speech-037 already did: "who errs, and
  comes short", "who knows the great enthusiasms" and "who know neither victory
  nor defeat". Both entries' notes name where the Theodore Roosevelt Center's
  transcription differs.
- **Grit & Perseverance**: grit-017 (Babe Ruth) is resourced to the earliest
  located form, the close of "Bat It Out!" under Ruth's byline in *The
  Rotarian* (July 1940), and grit-020 (Wilma Rudolph) to her autobiography,
  *Wilma* (1977), ch. 5. Both stay `attributed`.
- **Prophetic Wisdom**: hadith-025 now reads "those best to their wives", per
  Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1162, and hadith-027's source names both sayings it joins
  (Sahih al-Bukhari 6021 and 2989). The narrator and grading notes on
  hadith-005 and hadith-009 are corrected.
- **Hanukkah**: hanuk-001 now reads "the days of the dedication", per
  1 Maccabees 4:59 (RSV). hanuk-019 to hanuk-022 (Maimonides, Al HaNissim,
  Hanerot Halalu and the She-asa Nissim blessing) move from `verified` to
  `attributed`, because the English renderings could not be matched to a named
  translation.
